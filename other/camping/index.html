Overnight

One night out, everything on your back. Ticks save on this device; a print always comes out blank.

Tips

learned the hard way
  1. Weigh the loaded pack at home. Bathroom scales settle the argument the trail otherwise settles for you.
  2. Heavy things high and against your spine. Food and water central, sleeping bag at the bottom. A pack that pulls backwards makes you fight every step.
  3. Pitch a new tent in the backyard first. That is where a missing pole section is an annoyance rather than a night out.
  4. Sleep in clothes you have not walked in. Damp thermals cost more warmth than the bag’s rating gives back.
  5. Fill up at the last water you have actually seen. A creek drawn on a map is not a creek running in February.
  6. Cook and eat away from where you sleep. Food, rubbish, and toothpaste live away from the tent overnight.
  7. Pump the mat up full. Firm enough that your hip never finds the ground through it.
  8. Plan for colder than the forecast. A clear sky and a valley floor each pull the night below the number.

How it behaves

Tick things as they go in the pack. State lives in this browser and nowhere else, so the phone you pack with is the phone that remembers. Unpack all clears it for the next trip and asks twice.

Anything tagged optional is the first thing to cut when the pack gets heavy, and worn means it walks out the door on you rather than in the bag.

Rules of thumb

Under about 12 kg with food and water is a comfortable overnight load. The sleeping bag is rated to the forecast low, not to the name of the season.

The snake bandage and the beacon are the Australian entries. Neither weighs enough to argue about.
