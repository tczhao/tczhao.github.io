Tip 06 · when something is broken

Dead Air

Ten messages to send while something is broken and nobody yet knows why, each carried through to the reply you will get and what you say to that. The bug gets fixed on its own schedule. What you control is whether the person waiting on it feels informed or ignored, and almost all of that is decided in the hours when you have nothing to report.

This is not incident management. It is the writing that runs alongside it, usually done by whoever is closest to the customer while somebody else reads logs. Andon is the daily practice of saying the unwelcome thing in time. This is the version you do under load, in public, to someone who is already annoyed.

/

Standing and limit

Same standing as the rest of this section: craft, not evidence. There is a real literature on incident response and it is almost entirely about the response - detection, ownership, the postmortem afterwards - rather than the sentences sent alongside it while the response is still going. Nothing above carries a number I would be willing to put next to it. Treat it the way the section treats everything: worth doing, not worth citing.

The limit is that a cadence cannot carry a fix that is not coming. Updates on a schedule, in front of a problem nobody is actually working, are a better-organised way of losing somebody, and they buy about one incident’s worth of patience before people learn what the schedule is worth. Everything here assumes the work is real and the writing is the part that is missing.

The cost is also not carried evenly. The person who ends up writing these is often the most junior one in the thread, and “I do not have a date I would believe” is a much cheaper sentence to say with ten years behind you than with one. If you are the senior person there, the useful move is not to coach the update. It is to post the first one yourself, so everyone watching sees what it costs you to say you do not know.

Very little of the framing here is originally mine. It came out of an escalation I was part of and did not handle well, and the sharpest parts of it were written by a colleague in the thread afterwards. It qualifies for this section on the usual grounds: I got it wrong first.
