Reserve Price

Ten moves a professional seller will make on you, why each one works, and the line that answers it. Cars, property, trades, quotes - the moves are the same and only the numbers change.

Ask Price is the other side of this table, where you are selling your own labour. Here you are buying, the leverage runs the other way, and the difference is that they do this every day and you do it about six times in your life.

Standing and limit

Same standing as the rest of this section: craft, not evidence. The literature on negotiation is thin and contested and nothing above carries a number I would be willing to source. What is arithmetic is that on one large purchase the gap between a good afternoon and a bad one is often wider than a year of the fee optimisation Tare spends eight tracks on, and it is the only reason this page earns a place beside that one.

None of it works on a seller who genuinely does not need to sell. Try it there and you simply become the buyer they get to last. It also does not belong anywhere near someone who is not doing this professionally: a sole trader pricing a day of their own work is not running the drip on you, they are guessing, and grinding them down mostly buys you the version of the job that matches the price you pushed them to.

Every line here is meant to be said pleasantly. The technique is the content, not the tone. Being difficult is not the same as being firm, and it hands the other side a reason to prefer the next buyer.
