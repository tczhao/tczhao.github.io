China

The universal two-child policy takes effect

An amended Population and Family Planning Law took effect, allowing married couples nationwide to have two children. It replaced the previous one-child framework, which had included numerous exceptions.

Explore context & reflections

Context & interpretation

The policy responded to an aging population and a slowing supply of workers. It relaxed birth restrictions while retaining state limits on family size; it did not create unrestricted reproductive choice.

Consequences

Families gained more legal room to have a second child. The later move to three children and continuing population decline show that permission alone did not establish a sustained demographic turnaround.

General reflection

Birth policy can change quickly, while family decisions reflect longer-lived constraints: housing, care, education, employment and gender expectations.

Affected groups

2025 closes with reported growth and persistent imbalances

China’s National Bureau of Statistics reported preliminary real GDP growth of 5.0% for 2025. Its annual communiqué attributed 2.6 percentage points to final consumption, 0.8 to capital formation and 1.6 to net exports of goods and services.

Context & interpretation

These are official preliminary national accounts published in 2026 for activity in 2025. The World Bank’s December 2025 estimate was 4.9%; an earlier forecast is not the same observation as the later official estimate. Aggregate GDP does not summarize household welfare or every sector’s performance.

Consequences

The year-end figures show how exports supported reported growth while the World Bank continued to emphasize the property downturn and weak domestic demand as structural concerns.

General reflection

A headline growth rate is a starting point for analysis. Its composition, distribution and measurement status matter for understanding how an economy feels to households and firms.
