Entry 01 · The axle

On 1 November I fitted two new CV axles to the Forester, the shafts that drive its wheels. I'd planned on an hour: undo the axle nut, push the axle out of the wheel hub, fit the new one. It took four.

The axle had rusted into the hub. In the end I took the wheel hub out so I could lube it and hammer it at a good angle. The Forester is sixteen years old, and the three extra hours all went on that one stuck axle.

On an old car, the part tells you very little about how long a job will take. What decides it is how much rust is holding the part in, and you don't find that out until you start taking it apart.

I didn't stop when the hour ran out. I kept going because I wanted it done, and that has been my mindset.

Entry 02

Two bolts

When I unbolted the Tiguan's DPF mount and intake pipe bracket, each was missing a bolt. The DPF is the filter in a diesel's exhaust. The Tiguan is a 2008 and came to me second hand, already old and with a lot of workshop jobs behind it. Somewhere in that history someone put those parts back without their bolts, and nobody knew.

I don't think whoever did it was careless. Mechanics are people, and people make mistakes. So do I. But a workshop gets one go at a job, and then the car leaves. At home the car is still there next time, and I can keep coming back to it.

Entry 03

A timer, not a deadline

My day job is computer work, and it already runs on deadlines. An ETA I won't stop at is just another deadline, and bringing one home doubles the pressure. The garage is meant to balance out the week.

So I'm going to set a timer instead. When it goes off, I stop wherever the job has got to. If the car isn't finished, it waits.

The Tiguan shows I can afford to leave a car unfinished. It came off the road on 5 August at 239,342 km. On 30 October, 86 days later, it was still at 239,342 km. The log for those weeks has a DPF clean, balance shaft and oil pump parts, and new bolts for the balance shaft, harmonic balancer, tailshaft coupling, front subframe and dogbone mount.

This only works because the Tiguan isn't my only car. If it's the car you need on Monday, the timer turns back into a deadline.

About the numbers

Every number on this page comes from my maintenance log: 174 jobs across five cars since March 2021, each with its cost and, where I recorded them, a date and an odometer reading. The charts use the 137 jobs that have a date, grouped into 72 days of work. The 37 parts I logged without a date are left out. The hours for the Forester's axle aren't in the log.
